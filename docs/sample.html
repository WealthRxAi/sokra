Ways to lower this bill — biggest first

1. Charity care — IRS 501(r) financial assistance

$28,000–$40,000

Nonprofit hospital. $36,000 for 2 people ≈ 170% of the Federal Poverty Level; most 501(r) policies write off 100% up to 200% FPL.

  1. Call Patient Financial Services and request the Financial Assistance Policy application.
  2. Ask for a hold on the account while it's reviewed.
  3. Submit with 2 recent pay stubs, last tax return, 2 bank statements, proof of household size.
  4. Get the decision in writing. If denied, appeal — denials are often reversed.

Hi, I'm calling about account ending 4471, date of service September 17. I'd like to apply for your Financial Assistance Program under your 501(r) policy. Please send me the application, place a hold on this account while it's reviewed, and send a fully itemized bill with CPT codes. I'm not agreeing to a payment plan until the application is decided.

Who to contact: Patient Financial Services — (813) 555-0190

$1,800–$4,000

Three visible issues already: a duplicate supplies line, an OR quantity that doesn't match a typical appendectomy, and an unitemized pharmacy charge.

So you never need this again

Why does it matter that the hospital is a nonprofit?

Nonprofit hospitals don't pay most taxes. In exchange, federal law — IRS section 501(r) — requires them to have a written financial assistance policy and to tell patients about it. Most don't advertise it, but they can't refuse to give you the application.

How do I know if I qualify?

Almost always it's your household income compared to the Federal Poverty Level. For two people the 2026 FPL is about $21,150. Many hospitals write off 100% up to double that, and a sliding scale up to three or four times.

Why ask for an itemized bill before paying anything?

A summary bill hides the line items. Roughly 80% of hospital bills contain at least one error, and you can only dispute what you can see.

What does the 120-day window mean?

Most hospitals send unpaid accounts to a collection agency around 120 days after service. Once that happens, the hospital's own programs are harder to apply, and your leverage drops. Act inside the window.
